02 / THE SCENARIO

Mission briefing

A cluster of people in the fictional city of Northbridge develops fever, cough, and tiredness. Clinics report more patients than usual.

1 · Investigate

Collect symptom and exposure histories with qualified health professionals.

2 · Track

Monitor trends and identify possible links using privacy-protecting data.

3 · Respond

Share clear advice, support testing and care, and evaluate whether measures work.

This is an educational simulation, not a real disease alert or diagnosis.

04 / INVESTIGATION

How an outbreak is investigated

  1. Detect: Clinics notice an unusual increase in similar illness.
  2. Verify: Health authorities check reports and arrange appropriate testing.
  3. Describe: Compare cases by time, place, and person.
  4. Form hypotheses: Look for shared exposures without assuming the cause.
  5. Act and review: Apply proportionate controls and monitor results.
Evidence-based decisions change as new information becomes available.

07 / PREVENTION

Disease prevention & biosecurity

Everyday settings

Wash hands, improve indoor ventilation, stay home when ill where feasible, and follow current local health advice.

Healthcare

Use risk-appropriate protective equipment, hand hygiene, cleaning, and patient separation when indicated.

Community

Give clear, non-stigmatizing information; make testing and care accessible; correct rumors quickly.

Choose measures based on how the disease spreads and the available evidence.

08 / BIOTECHNOLOGY

From sample to answer

  1. Collect: A trained professional collects an appropriate sample.
  2. Test: A validated diagnostic method looks for evidence of infection.
  3. Interpret: Qualified staff review results alongside symptoms and exposure.
  4. Respond: Results guide care and public-health action.

Other biotechnology tools

  • Genomic sequencing can help compare pathogen samples and identify variants.
  • Vaccine research may help prevent disease, depending on the pathogen and evidence.
  • Laboratory quality controls reduce misleading results.
09 / ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

AI tracking systems

Input

De-identified case counts, symptom-onset dates, test results, and broad location data.

Analysis

Find unusual increases, map possible clusters, and compare trends over time.

Human review

Experts check data quality, context, and alternative explanations.

Safeguards

  • Protect privacy and limit access.
  • Check for bias and missing data.
  • A flagged pattern does not prove causation.
  • Qualified people remain accountable for decisions.
10 / CONTACT TRACING

Supportive follow-up

Contact tracing helps health teams identify people who may have been exposed and connect them with appropriate advice, testing, or care.

1 · Identify

Interview cases respectfully and confidentially.

2 · Inform

Explain possible exposure and health-authority recommendations.

3 · Follow up

Offer support and monitor symptoms when appropriate.

The approach depends on transmission route, resources, and current guidance. It should never involve public shaming.

11 / ACTION PLAN

Our response plan

First 24 hours

Verify reports, alert the responsible health authority, protect healthcare staff, and communicate what is known.

Next 2–3 days

Coordinate testing and case investigation; review capacity; share consistent public information.

Ongoing

Update the dashboard, assess whether interventions work, and adjust actions to new evidence.

Success indicators: faster detection, safer care, reliable reporting, public trust, and fewer new infections.
